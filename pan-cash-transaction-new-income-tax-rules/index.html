Cash Transactions Up to ₹10 Lakh No Longer Require PAN

Effective from April 1, 2026 | New Income Tax Act, 2025

The Government of India is preparing to introduce sweeping reforms under the New Income Tax Act, 2025, aimed at simplifying compliance, reducing unnecessary paperwork, and focusing tax monitoring on high-value transactions. One of the most talked-about changes is the relaxation of PAN requirements for cash transactions.

According to the draft provisions, individuals will no longer be required to provide their Permanent Account Number (PAN) for cash deposits or withdrawals up to ₹10 lakh in a financial year. This move marks a major departure from the existing rules and is expected to bring relief to millions of small taxpayers and business owners.

Why Has the Government Introduced These Changes?

Over the years, the government has steadily tightened financial regulations to curb black money, tax evasion, and unaccounted cash dealings. While these measures helped strengthen the tax ecosystem, they also increased compliance pressure on honest taxpayers, especially those involved in small businesses or cash-based trades.

The New Income Tax Act, 2025 seeks to strike a balance. The idea is simple: monitor big money aggressively while easing rules for small, routine transactions. By doing so, the government aims to promote ease of doing business and encourage financial inclusion.

Key Objective: Reduce compliance burden on small taxpayers while strengthening oversight on high-value financial activities.

Cash Deposit and Withdrawal Rules Explained

Under the proposed framework, PAN will be mandatory only if total cash deposits or withdrawals exceed ₹10 lakh in a single financial year. Currently, PAN is required even if a person deposits more than ₹50,000 in cash in a single day, which many consider excessive for small traders and salaried individuals.

This change is expected to benefit:

  • Small shopkeepers and traders
  • Rural and semi-urban account holders
  • Individuals dealing in seasonal or cash-based income
  • Senior citizens with limited digital access

Changes in PAN Rules for Hotels and Events

Another major reform relates to hospitality and event-related expenses. The government has proposed increasing the PAN requirement limit for hotel stays and services from ₹50,000 to ₹1 lakh.

This means hotel bills below ₹1 lakh will no longer require PAN details. The same threshold will apply to payments made to convention centers, banquet halls, and event management companies.

This step is expected to boost domestic tourism and reduce inconvenience for individuals attending weddings, conferences, and social events.

Property Transactions: Higher PAN Threshold

Property transactions often involve extensive documentation and compliance. Under the proposed changes, PAN will be mandatory only if the property value exceeds ₹20 lakh, compared to the existing limit of ₹10 lakh.

This change is particularly relevant for small towns and semi-urban areas, where property prices are still relatively moderate.

New PAN Rules for Vehicle Purchases

The draft rules also introduce clarity in vehicle purchase regulations. PAN will now be mandatory only if a person purchases:

  • A motorcycle costing more than ₹5 lakh
  • Any motor vehicle priced above ₹5 lakh

Earlier, PAN was mandatory for all four-wheelers irrespective of cost, while two-wheelers were completely exempt. The new approach creates a fair, price-based system.

Cryptocurrency Transactions Under Tight Watch

While the government is relaxing PAN norms for small cash transactions, it is simultaneously tightening surveillance on digital and virtual assets. Cryptocurrency exchanges will now be required to share detailed transaction data with the Income Tax Department.

Digital currencies have been formally recognized as electronic payment instruments, bringing them under the broader tax reporting framework. This move aims to prevent misuse of crypto platforms for tax evasion or illegal transfers.

Impact on Small Businesses and Common Taxpayers

For small business owners, these reforms could be game-changing. Reduced documentation means less time spent on compliance and more focus on growth. Individuals dealing with limited cash transactions will no longer fear penalties or scrutiny for minor deposits.

Experts believe these reforms will:

  • Improve voluntary tax compliance
  • Encourage transparent financial behavior
  • Reduce unnecessary interactions with tax authorities
  • Support ease of doing business initiatives

Implementation Timeline

Following the announcement in Budget 2026, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) has invited public feedback on the draft rules. After reviewing stakeholder suggestions, the final rules are expected to be notified by early March.

The New Income Tax Act, 2025 along with these PAN-related changes will come into effect from April 1, 2026.

Final Thoughts

The proposed reforms mark a significant shift in India’s tax administration philosophy. By easing compliance for small transactions and strengthening oversight for larger ones, the government is attempting to create a more balanced, efficient, and taxpayer-friendly system.

As the implementation date approaches, taxpayers are advised to stay informed and consult professionals for clarity on how these changes may impact their finances.
